Welcome to my website! I am a 6th-year PhD candidate in the [Sustainable Development program](https://www.sipa.columbia.edu/sipa-education/phd-sustainable-development) at Columbia University's School of International and Public Affairs and Climate School. My fields are environmental economics and labor economics and my research interests lie at the intersection of extreme weather adaptation and labor and household credit markets. **I am on the 2025-2026 job market.**

In my [job market paper](https://hannahfarkas.github.io/files/Unpredictable_by_design_website.pdf), I investigate the schedule unpredictability of hourly workers in service sectors, and how this responds to minimum wage laws and extreme weather. In other work, I study the political processes behind the systems through which we allocate aid following natural disasters, the adaptive capacity of hourly workers to respond to wildfire smoke, and how credit scores and financial wellbeing are impacted by water utility bills in drought-burdened areas of California.

Prior to joining Columbia University, I served as a Research Assistant at the Federal Reserve Board in Washington, D.C. in the Consumer Finance Section. During my tenure there, I studied aspects of household auto loan debt, credit card debt, student loan debt, and home mortgage markets, particularly tracking the differences in characteristics of debt across subprime and prime borrowers, driving my interest in studying the impacts of climate change on consumer debt and household financial wellbeing.

I received my BA from Johns Hopkins University in 2017, where I graduated Phi Beta Kappa with a double major in Global Environmental Change and Sustainability and Economics. In my free time, I love hiking, baking cakes, running, and exploring all of NYC's many concert venues.

Please reach out if you are interested in my research!
